## intro | The big idea

@flow
Start | Scarcity | Not enough resources for everything people want.
Forced by it | Choice | You have to pick.
Result | Opportunity cost | What you gave up by picking.

@def: **Economics** is how people use scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants.
@ex: Toronto housing. Land is limited, many people want it, so people pay more or live farther out.
@ex[Class example · Applying Concepts 1-1]: **The cost of a degree.** Tuition plus the wages you don't earn.
@trap: Money is not "capital" in this course. Capital means tools and equipment.
@mem: LLC: **L**and, **L**abour, **C**apital.
@tip[Heads up]: The exam asks you to *find* the opportunity cost, not only define it.
@biz: A company with spare cash can buy back shares or build a warehouse. Picking one costs the other.
@math: 9 × $1m + 6 × $0.5m = $12m ✓
@say[Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations*]: "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher..."

@worked What does a year of college cost?
prob: Ana can study ($10,000 tuition) or work ($38,000). Rent is $14,000 either way. What is the cost of studying?
1. Add what she pays because of the choice. :: Tuition: $10,000.
2. Add what she gives up. :: Wages: $38,000.
3. Ignore costs she pays anyway. :: Rent is the same either way.
ans: About **$48,000**. || why: Counting only tuition makes the degree look five times cheaper.
try: **Your turn.** A $6,000 scholarship. New cost? || $4,000 + $38,000 = **$42,000**.

## selftest.selftest | Self-test
sub: 4 questions. Try each before you reveal it.

? mcq | 1 | factors
Which of these is a **factor of production**?
- The $5,000 in a company's bank account
+ A CN Rail locomotive
- A share of Shopify stock
> A locomotive is **capital**. Money and shares are financial claims, not tools.

? multi | 2 | factors | src=Pearson style
~ Pearson style
Select all the factors of production.
+ Labour
+ Land
- Money in a bank account
> Land, labour and capital are the three factors.

? num | 2 | opportunity cost | answer=1.5 | tol=0.01 | unit=coffees
60 coffees or 40 sandwiches an hour. What does **one sandwich** cost, in coffees?
> 60 ÷ 40 = **1.5 coffees**.

? short | 2 | scarcity
Explain why scarcity forces a choice.
> **Model answer:** Wants are unlimited and resources are not, so picking one thing means giving up another.
