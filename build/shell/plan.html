Study a chapter

Pick a chapter, then a way to study it. Each way is a loop of 3 steps. These are ways to study, not types of learner: anyone can use any of them, so pick by what the chapter needs.

Why these ways, and not "learning styles"
  • No learner types. Studies that tested "I am a visual (or auditory) learner" found that matching how you study to that label does not improve results. What helps is the method.
  • Every loop makes you pull answers from memory. Quizzing yourself beats rereading, but it feels harder, so it can feel like it is not working. Trust the scores, not the feeling.
  • Every loop says come back tomorrow. Short sessions on different days beat one long cram.
  • Examples help most when a topic is new. Once you get them right easily, skip the example and just do questions.
Based on Pashler et al. 2008, Karpicke et al. 2009, Kalyuga et al. 2001, Carpenter et al. 2022 and Tetzlaff et al. 2025.

A weekly rhythm

In classOpen Notes (bottom right). Type fast, mark exam hints with ★ and anything unclear with ?.
Same dayRead the chapter once. Do the worked examples with the steps hidden.
Day 1Self-test, no peeking. Turn each ★ from your notes into a question you can answer.
Day 3 and 7Hit Review now. Only the questions that are due come up.
Week of the testRun Exam mode with every chapter mixed, so you practise telling topics apart.
Why mix chapters Shuffling topics together is harder than studying one chapter at a time, and that difficulty is what helps you tell similar ideas apart on a real test. Use single chapters while learning, mixed mode when reviewing.
