The study method

Five habits that have the strongest research backing, and where each one lives in this map.

1 · Test yourself

Quiz before you re-read

Pulling an answer out of your head beats reading the page again. Practice tests beat restudying across studies, and the gain still shows up a week later on new application questions when you do several rounds. It only works if you really try first, so write or say your answer before you reveal it.

Here: the Self-test at the end of every chapter and Exam mode.

Evidence: Adesope et al. 2017 (meta-analysis of practice testing), Corral et al. 2025, Endres et al. 2015 (it helps when it takes effort).
2 · Space it out

Come back on day 1, 3, 7, 14

Four short sessions beat one long cram. Classroom studies show a medium-sized gain for spaced practice, bigger when the gap before the test is longer. The "Due for review" counter does the scheduling for you.

Here: the due counter above and the Plan chapter bars.

Evidence: Mawson et al. 2025 (classroom meta-analysis, d = 0.54), Donoghue & Hattie 2021 (practice testing and spacing ranked best of ten techniques).
3 · Use concrete examples

Pin every idea to a real case

Abstract definitions stick better with concrete, real-world examples. After each definition, ask "what's one real company or situation where I'd see this?" The green Example boxes and the Business angle boxes are there for that.

Here: Example and Business angle callouts in every chapter.

Evidence: Micallef et al. 2022 (replication: concrete examples help learning of abstract concepts).
4 · Work examples step by step

Study solved problems, then try one

When a topic is new, studying a worked solution teaches more than jumping into problems cold. Cover the answer, attempt it, then reveal one step at a time and explain to yourself why each step is there. Finish with the "Your turn" problem.

Here: the Worked example blocks.

Evidence: Barbieri et al. 2023 (worked examples, g = 0.48), Atkinson et al. 2003 (fading steps + explaining the principle).
5 · Make it routine

Lower the effort, keep the habit

Most students already know self-testing works; they skip it because it feels costly. So keep sessions short (15 to 25 minutes), start with the questions that are due, and stop after one chapter. Underlining and summarizing ranked lowest of the ten techniques in the big meta-analysis, so spend your time elsewhere.

Here: Review now button, Exam mode presets.

Evidence: Rea et al. 2022 (students recognize good strategies but don't use them), Weinstein et al. 2018 (six strategies review).

A simple loop for each chapter

In classOpen Notes (bottom right). Type fast, mark exam hints with ★ and anything unclear with ?.
Same dayRead the chapter once. Do the worked examples with the steps hidden.
Day 1Self-test, no peeking. Turn each ★ from your notes into a question you can answer.
Day 3 and 7Hit Review now. Only the questions that are due come up.
Week of the testRun Exam mode with every chapter mixed, so you practise telling topics apart.
Why mix chapters Shuffling topics together is harder than studying one chapter at a time, and that difficulty is what helps you tell similar ideas apart on a real test. Use single chapters while learning, mixed mode when reviewing.
